Money gets you in the poll. It doesn’t decide where you land.

We compared estimated 2026 roster budgets for 68 programs with the AP Top 25. All ten of the biggest spenders are ranked. So are Houston, Iowa and Utah, on budgets below the median.

Ranked teams spend about $13M more than everyone else

Each dot is a program: further right means a bigger budget, higher up means a better AP rank. Hollow dots are unranked and sit in the band at the bottom.

Line: least-squares fit among the 25 ranked teams. Shaded band: 95% confidence interval for the line.

A $10M bigger budget goes with about five times the odds of being ranked

The trendline above only covers teams that made the poll. This curve uses all 68 teams: ranked teams are the dots along the top, unranked teams along the bottom, and the line is the fitted chance of being ranked at each budget. Houston, on about $17M, had a fitted chance of about 8% and made the poll. Florida, on about $36M, had about 70% and didn’t.

What these numbers can’t tell you

  • Correlation, not cause. Winning also raises budgets through donations and revenue, and prestige, recruiting and coaching affect both.
  • The line fits ranked teams only. AP ranks exist for 25 teams, so the slope says nothing about how unranked teams would order, and cutting off at 25 tends to flatten it. The ranked-versus-unranked comparison uses all 68.
  • Small sample, not fully independent. It is one season. Teams in the same conference, and teams that play each other, aren’t independent, so the p-values and intervals are somewhat optimistic.
  • Estimated budgets. Random noise in the estimates usually weakens a real relationship. A systematic bias in the estimates wouldn’t show up in the range test.
  • Not a random sample of programs. These 68 teams are mostly power-conference schools, so the results don’t describe all of college football.
  • Exploratory. These checks were chosen after looking at the data, with no correction for multiple tests. They all ask the same question and the main p-values are far below 0.05, but treat the exact values loosely.
