Where Europe's platform nights go

Eurostat's article maps the last three months of 2025 (October to December, or “Q4”), when Andalucía, the Canary Islands and the Paris region (Île-de-France) led. The full year of monthly data tells a bigger story: the hot spots move with the seasons, and a quarter's map shows only one of them.

Platform nights
One guest staying one night in a short-term rental (a holiday flat, house or room, not a hotel) booked through Airbnb, Booking.com or Expedia.
Region
Eurostat's standard mid-sized areas, called “NUTS 2” regions: roughly a province or state, such as Andalucía, Lazio or Upper Bavaria. The EU has 242 of them.
Q4
The fourth quarter of the year: October, November and December. It is the period the Eurostat article maps.
Per resident
Nights divided by the number of people who live in the region, a way to compare a small island with a big city.

    Data. Eurostat, nights spent in short-term rentals (holiday flats, houses and rooms, not hotels) booked through Airbnb, Booking.com or Expedia Group, by month in 2025, as published in the MakeoverMonday dataset. Q4 is October to December. The page uses the 27 EU countries' regions at Eurostat's “NUTS 2” level; Iceland, Liechtenstein, Norway and Switzerland are in the file but left out, as in the article's EU figures.

    Gaps. The file has no figures for Utrecht and Zuid-Holland in the Netherlands, or for Centro, the Lisbon area and Alentejo in Portugal. Their borders were redrawn in 2024; the file lists them under the old borders with no numbers, and the new regions are missing. They are shown as “no data”.

    Population and boundaries. Nights per resident uses each region's population on 1 January 2025 from Eurostat's population table. Region borders are Eurostat's official map of regions (2024 edition, simplified for screen; © EuroGeographics for the administrative boundaries). The file names regions but gives no codes, so each name was matched to Eurostat's region code by name within its country. Islands far from the mainland (Spain's Canary Islands, Portugal's Azores and Madeira, and France's overseas regions in the Caribbean, South America and the Indian Ocean) are drawn in small boxes below the map, not to scale.
